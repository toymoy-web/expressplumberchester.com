Basement Flooding? Check the Sump Pump First

A sump pump that isn't running is one of the most common reasons a basement floods during heavy rain, and it usually fails for one of a few reasons: a stuck float switch, a clogged intake screen, a worn-out motor, or a power outage with no battery backup in place. Chester's proximity to the Delaware River and its share of older basements with limited grading around the foundation make sump pump reliability a genuinely practical concern here, not just a nice-to-have. This same vintage shows up across Chester, from the Sun Village neighborhood to the streets near Widener University.

A pump that runs constantly, rather than cycling on and off as expected, is also a real problem: it points to a stuck switch or a pump that's undersized for how much water is actually reaching the pit. A pit that fills faster than the pump can keep up with during a heavy storm is a sizing issue worth addressing before the next major rain event rather than after a flooded basement.

2

Repair or Replace

Stuck Float Switch

Often the cause of a pump that won't turn on at all; a straightforward part replacement in most cases.

Worn Motor or Impeller

A pump running but not actually moving water, or making a grinding sound, usually needs the pump unit replaced rather than repaired.

Battery Backup Addition

A pump with no backup power fails exactly when it's needed most, during a storm-related power outage; adding battery backup addresses that specific gap.

Why Battery Backup Matters

The scenario that causes the most basement flood damage isn't a broken pump, it's a working pump that loses power during the exact storm that's testing it. A battery backup system keeps the pump running for hours during an outage, which is often the entire difference between a dry basement and a flooded one after a major storm passes through.

What to Do If Your Basement Is Flooding Right Now

  • Avoid entering standing water near an electrical outlet, extension cord, or the sump pump's own power connection.
  • Shut off electricity to the affected area at the breaker if you can do so without stepping into water.
  • Move electronics, stored boxes, and valuables off the floor and away from the rising water line.
  • Call our 24/7 line; a technician can advise over the phone while you wait if the pump itself needs immediate attention.

Signs Your Sump Pump Needs Attention

  • The pump doesn't turn on when water in the pit rises to the normal trigger level.
  • A grinding, rattling, or unusually loud sound during operation.
  • The pump runs constantly rather than cycling on and off.
  • Visible rust or corrosion on the pump or its discharge pipe.
  • No battery backup in place for power outages during storms.

Sizing a Sump Pump Correctly

A pump that's undersized for how much groundwater actually reaches the pit during heavy rain will run constantly and wear out faster, while an oversized pump cycles too frequently and can also shorten its own lifespan. Pit size, discharge line length, and how much water Chester's older basements typically see during a storm all factor into picking the right pump rather than defaulting to whatever unit happens to be in stock.

A check valve on the discharge line is worth confirming during any pump service too, since a missing or failed check valve lets water flow back into the pit after each cycle, making the pump work harder and more often than it should. A discharge line that empties too close to the foundation can also send the same water right back toward the basement, so where the line actually terminates outside gets checked along with the pump itself.

What Sump Pump Service Costs

Frequently Asked Questions

Why didn't my sump pump turn on during a storm?

The most common causes are a stuck float switch, a clogged intake screen, or a power outage with no battery backup in place. All three are checked during a service call to confirm the actual cause.

Is a battery backup sump pump worth it?

Yes, in most cases. The scenario that causes the most flood damage is a pump losing power during the same storm that's testing it, and a battery backup keeps the pump running through that outage.

How long do sump pumps typically last?

Most sump pumps last 7 to 10 years with normal use. A pump running constantly, making unusual noise, or showing visible corrosion is a candidate for replacement even before it fails completely.

Do you offer 24/7 emergency sump pump repair?

Yes, particularly during active storms when a failed pump puts a basement at immediate risk of flooding.
