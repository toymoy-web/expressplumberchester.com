Cold Snaps Hit Eddystone's Housing Stock Hard

Eddystone is a small borough of about 2,459 people on the Delaware River, and its housing is genuinely distinct from anywhere else in our service area: much of it was built as company housing for workers at the Baldwin Locomotive Works, once the largest steam locomotive manufacturer in the world, and the earlier Eddystone Print Works textile mill, both clustered here starting in the 1870s and 1880s. That tenement-era housing, now well over a century old, tends to have less insulation and more exposed pipe runs in basements and crawlspaces than housing built even a few decades later, which makes a hard winter cold snap a genuinely bigger risk here than in newer construction nearby.

A burst pipe after a freeze is one of our most common Eddystone calls for exactly this reason: an exposed line in an under-insulated century-old basement cracks during the cold snap and the leak often isn't discovered until things thaw and pressure returns.

If You Have a Burst Pipe or Active Leak

  • Shut off the main water valve if you can reach it safely.
  • Turn off electricity to any area with standing water before touching outlets or appliances there.
  • Take a few photos of the damage before cleanup starts, for a homeowners insurance claim.
  • Call our 24/7 emergency line; a technician will confirm arrival timing on the phone.

Winterizing Older Eddystone Homes

  • Insulate exposed pipes in basements and crawlspaces before the first hard freeze each year.
  • Know where your main shutoff valve is before you need it in an emergency.
  • Let faucets drip slightly during extreme cold to relieve pressure in exposed lines.
  • Disconnect and drain outdoor hoses and spigots before winter.

A Small Borough With a Big Industrial Past

At just over one square mile, Eddystone is easy to reach quickly from our Chester coverage area, and its housing, while old, is concentrated enough that a technician familiar with the borough's company-town construction style can generally diagnose an aging-pipe issue faster than on a first visit to unfamiliar housing stock. The Baldwin Locomotive Works site itself, now partly occupied by an Exelon generating station, is a useful landmark reference for confirming which side of the borough a specific address sits on when scheduling a visit. Eddystone's small size, just over a square mile, also means most of the borough's housing shares a similar age and construction style, so a diagnosis that fits one block is a reasonable starting assumption for the next one, confirmed on site rather than assumed sight unseen. That consistency also makes emergency response more predictable here than in a township with a wider mix of construction eras spread across a larger area, and it is a genuine reason a technician who has worked one Eddystone call is usually well prepared for the next one nearby.
