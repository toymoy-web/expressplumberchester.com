Water Line Repair & Replacement in Chester, PA

A wet spot in the yard, low pressure throughout the house, or no water at all can mean a break in the underground line running from the street to your home. Chester Express Plumbing repairs water service lines.

No Water or Low Pressure From the Street?

The water service line is the underground pipe running from the water main in the street to your home, separate from the interior supply lines and separate from the sewer lateral that carries wastewater out. A break or major leak in this line often shows up as a wet or unusually green patch of lawn between the street and the house, a sudden drop in water pressure throughout the entire house, or a complete loss of water with the meter still showing usage.

This is a different problem from a leak inside the house: the fix, permitting, and sometimes the responsible party (utility versus homeowner, depending on exactly where the break is) all differ from an interior repair.

2

Repair Options

Spot Excavation Repair

For a single, localized break, a targeted dig at that point repairs the line without disturbing the full run from street to house.

Trenchless Replacement

For an older line with multiple weak points, a new line can often be pulled through the old one's path without a full open trench across the yard.

Full Excavation Replacement

A severely deteriorated line, common with original galvanized service lines in Chester's oldest housing, is sometimes best replaced in full.

A Note on Older Service Line Materials

Cities built before the mid-20th century, Chester included, sometimes have original water service lines made of lead or galvanized steel rather than the copper or plastic used today, a concern many older U.S. cities and their water utilities have been actively addressing in recent years. If you're unsure what material your home's service line is made of, Chester's water utility or the Pennsylvania Department of Environmental Protection can help confirm it, and a technician can also visually inspect the exposed portion where it enters your home.

Signs You Have a Water Service Line Problem

  • A wet, soggy, or unusually green patch of lawn between the street and your house with no other explanation.
  • A sudden, unexplained drop in water pressure throughout the entire house.
  • The water meter running even when all fixtures inside are off.
  • A noticeably higher water bill with no change in usage.
  • No water at all, with the shutoff valves confirmed open.

Who Is Responsible: You or the Utility

The water service line has a property-line boundary that typically divides homeowner responsibility from the municipal utility's responsibility, though the exact split varies by municipality. A technician can help identify where a break sits relative to that boundary, which matters for figuring out whether the repair is a homeowner cost or something to report to Chester's water utility instead.

Older Chester properties with a shared driveway or a service line that crosses a neighboring property occasionally complicate this further, which is worth flagging early rather than discovering mid-repair. A permit is typically required for any excavation-based water line repair in Chester, the same as for a repipe, and that gets pulled before digging starts rather than treated as an afterthought.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I know if my water line, not my sewer line, is the problem?

A water line problem usually shows up as low pressure, a sudden loss of water, or a rising water bill with a wet spot in the yard. A sewer line problem shows up as drains backing up or sewage odor. The two are separate systems with different symptoms.

Do I have to dig up my whole yard to fix a water line?

Not always. A single, localized break can often be repaired with a spot excavation, and trenchless replacement can install a new line without a full open trench for a line with multiple weak points.

Could my home have a lead water service line?

It's possible in a city as old as Chester. Chester's water utility or the Pennsylvania Department of Environmental Protection can confirm your specific line's material, and a technician can visually inspect the exposed portion where the line enters your home.

Is a water line break an emergency?

A complete loss of water or a major leak flooding your yard is treated as an emergency call on our 24/7 line.
