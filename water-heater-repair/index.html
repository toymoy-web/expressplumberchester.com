Common Water Heater Problems We See in Chester

Water heater calls in Chester cluster around a small set of real problems: no hot water at all, a puddle forming at the base of the tank, a pilot light that will not stay lit, or rusty water coming from the hot side only.

A leak at the base of the tank, in particular, often shows up first near the edge of the unit where it meets the floor or a garage slab, and it rarely gets better on its own. Widener University's campus sits in the middle of this same older housing stock, built in the same early-1900s wave.

Many Chester homes still run older gas tank water heaters original to the property or installed decades ago, which means age-related tank failure is a more common cause here than it might be in newer construction elsewhere in the county.

Repair or Replace: How We Decide

Under 6 Years Old

A younger tank with a specific failed part, such as a thermocouple or heating element, is usually repaired rather than replaced.

8+ Years or Leaking at the Tank

A tank leaking from the tank body itself, not a fitting, has failed structurally and needs replacement, not a patch.

Tankless Units

Tankless water heaters are more often serviceable through descaling or a part replacement even past 10 years, since the tank itself cannot fail the way a tank unit can.

What a Same-Day Replacement Looks Like

Most tank replacements in Chester are completed the same day the technician arrives, including removal and haul-away of the old unit. Gas line and venting are checked against current code before the new unit is connected, which matters in older homes where original venting was not sized for today's higher-efficiency units.

For homes considering a switch from gas to electric or to a heat pump water heater, that conversation happens on site since it affects electrical capacity and installation cost, not just the unit price.

Emergency Signs You Need Water Heater Service Now

  • No hot water, or hot water that runs out much faster than it used to.
  • A puddle or damp area at the base of the tank.
  • Rust-colored water from hot taps only, not cold.
  • A rumbling or popping sound from the tank, often from sediment buildup.
  • A pilot light that will not stay lit, or a gas smell near the unit.

Gas Line and Venting Checks Matter in Older Homes

A water heater installed decades ago in a Chester rowhome was often sized and vented for the standards of its time, which is not always compatible with a modern high-efficiency replacement dropped in without changes. Venting diameter, combustion air supply, and gas line sizing all get checked against current code as part of a replacement, not assumed to already be correct because the old unit worked.

This check is also why a straightforward-looking swap can occasionally turn into a slightly larger job: if the existing venting or gas line does not meet code for the new unit, that gets addressed and priced before installation, not discovered after the fact.

Water quality plays a role too. Older galvanized supply lines feeding a water heater can shed sediment into the tank faster than copper or PEX does, which shortens tank life and is worth mentioning if a replacement tank seems to fail sooner than its rated lifespan suggests it should.

Gas Safety: What to Do Before We Arrive

  • If you smell gas near the water heater, leave the house immediately and call from outside. Do not flip light switches or use anything that could spark on your way out.
  • Turn off the gas supply valve at the tank only if you can do so without lingering near a suspected leak.
  • Do not attempt to relight a pilot light yourself if it has gone out repeatedly. A repeatedly failing pilot can signal a venting or gas supply problem that needs a technician.
  • Keep the area around the water heater clear of stored items, since good airflow matters for both gas combustion units and safe technician access.

A Leaking Water Heater Is an Emergency

Water pooling around the tank is the most common emergency water heater call. Shut off the cold water supply to the tank, turn the heater to off or pilot, and call our 24/7 line. A tank leaking from the shell is corroded through and cannot be repaired, so replacement is the fix. Leaks from a fitting or relief valve can often be repaired.

A technician finds the source, tells you whether repair or replacement makes sense, and gives an upfront price before work starts. Same-day replacement is common for standard tank units.

No Hot Water

No hot water usually comes from a failed heating element, thermostat, pilot or igniter, or a tank that has reached the end of its life. We diagnose the cause on the first visit. Tankless units that lose hot water often need descaling or a part, so see our tankless water heater service page.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do you replace water heaters the same day in Chester?

In most cases, yes. A technician can remove the old unit and install a new tank water heater in one visit, including haul-away of the old tank.

Is it worth repairing an older water heater or should I replace it?

A tank under about 6 years old with a single failed part is usually worth repairing. A tank 8 years or older, or one leaking from the tank body itself, has failed structurally and needs replacement.

Do you work on tankless water heaters?

Yes, including descaling, flow sensor and igniter issues, and full tankless replacement.

Do you offer 24/7 emergency water heater repair?

Yes. No hot water and active leaks at the tank are both treated as emergency calls on our 24/7 line.

Is a leaking water heater an emergency?

Yes. Shut off the cold water supply to the tank and call (610) 672-7601 any hour. A tank leaking from the shell must be replaced, and we can often do that the same day.

What if I have no hot water in the middle of the night?

Call our 24/7 line. We diagnose the heater and fix or replace it, with an upfront price before work starts.
