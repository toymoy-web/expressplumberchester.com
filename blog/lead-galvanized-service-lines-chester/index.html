Lead vs. Galvanized Service Lines: What Older Chester Homes Should Know

A real, national concern for cities built before the mid-20th century. Here's what the difference actually is and how to find out what your home has.

By the Chester Express Plumbing team · Serving Chester, PA

Why This Comes Up in Cities as Old as Chester

Chester is the oldest incorporated city in Pennsylvania, and a real share of its housing dates to the late 1800s and early 1900s. Cities built in that era, across the country, sometimes have original water service lines, the underground pipe running from the municipal water main to the house, made of lead or galvanized steel rather than the copper or plastic used in modern construction. This is a genuine, well-documented national concern that many older U.S. cities and their water utilities have been actively working to address in recent years, not something specific to or invented for Chester.

Lead pipe was actually used as a water-line material before galvanized steel became standard, precisely because lead was durable and easy to work with. It fell out of favor once the health risks of lead exposure through drinking water became well understood.

How to Tell the Difference

Lead

Dull gray, soft enough to be scratched with a coin, and not magnetic. Often has a slightly rounded, bulged look at joints.

Galvanized Steel

Also dull gray, but harder, magnetic, and typically has visible threaded joints rather than the smoother, wiped-solder joints common on lead pipe.

Copper

A distinct reddish-brown color when unweathered, sometimes greenish if it has patina. Not magnetic. The modern standard for decades.

Confirming What Your Home Actually Has

  • Locate where the water service line enters your home, usually in a basement or utility area near the water meter.
  • Check the pipe material at that entry point using the visual and magnet tests above.
  • Contact Chester's water utility or the Pennsylvania Department of Environmental Protection for any records they may have on service line materials in your area.
  • If you're unsure or can't access the entry point, a plumber can inspect and confirm during a routine visit.

What to Do If You Find Lead or Aging Galvanized Pipe

Finding a lead or galvanized service line is not, by itself, an emergency. Many homes with older service lines have safe water due to treatment practices at the utility level, but the only way to fully eliminate any risk from a lead line specifically is full replacement, since even after treatment, corrosion inside old galvanized pipe can periodically release lead that had been trapped there from an earlier lead connection or fitting.

If replacement makes sense for your situation, our water line repair team can discuss trenchless and traditional replacement options, and our water filtration service can help address water quality in the meantime if a full line replacement isn't happening immediately.

Why Galvanized Pipe Adds a Second Layer to This

Even a home that never had a lead service line specifically can still have a lead-adjacent issue if it runs galvanized steel pipe: older galvanized fittings and connectors sometimes contained lead components, and decades of internal corrosion in galvanized pipe can trap small amounts of that lead, releasing it intermittently into water flow long after any original lead fitting was removed elsewhere in the system. This is part of why full replacement, not just removing an obvious lead section, is the only way to fully close the door on this specific risk.

None of this is meant to alarm anyone with an older Chester home. Millions of households across the country live with older infrastructure safely, thanks in large part to water treatment practices at the utility level. It is simply worth knowing your own home's actual materials rather than guessing, since that information changes what, if anything, is worth prioritizing.

Simple Steps to Reduce Exposure While You Decide

  • Run the cold tap for a minute before using water for drinking or cooking, especially first thing in the morning, if you have any concern about your service line material.
  • Use cold water for drinking and cooking, since hot water dissolves more material from pipes than cold water does.
  • Consider a certified water filter rated for lead reduction as an interim step if a full line replacement is not happening right away.
  • Have your water tested by an accredited lab if you want a direct answer about current lead levels rather than relying on pipe material alone.
  • Avoid using water from a tap that has sat unused for several hours or more for drinking or cooking without first flushing it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Chester still have lead water service lines in some homes?

It's genuinely possible in a city this old, though we can't say for certain without inspecting a specific property. Chester's water utility or the Pennsylvania Department of Environmental Protection may have records, and a plumber can visually confirm the material at your home's service line entry point.

Is my water unsafe if I have a lead service line?

Municipal water treatment reduces risk, but the only way to fully eliminate any lead exposure risk from an old service line is to replace it. If you're concerned, a water test can give you a direct answer about what's actually in your water right now.

How can I tell lead pipe from galvanized pipe myself?

Lead is soft enough to scratch with a coin and is not magnetic. Galvanized steel is much harder and is magnetic. Both are typically a dull gray color, which is why the scratch and magnet tests matter more than color alone.

How much does it cost to replace a lead or galvanized service line?

Cost depends on the line's length, depth, and whether trenchless replacement is an option for your property. See our water line repair page for typical cost ranges, or call for a number specific to your address.
