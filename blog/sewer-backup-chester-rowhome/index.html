What to Do When a Sewer Backs Up in an Older Chester Rowhome

Sewer backups are more common in Chester's older, shared-lateral rowhomes than in newer construction. Here's what to do if it happens and why it happens here.

By the Chester Express Plumbing team · Serving Chester, PA

What to Do in the First Few Minutes

  • Avoid direct contact with backed-up sewage; treat any standing water from a backup as contaminated.
  • Do not run additional water down any drain, shower, or toilet while a backup is active.
  • Keep children and pets away from the affected area until it is fully cleared and cleaned.
  • Photograph the damage for insurance purposes before cleanup starts, but avoid wading through standing sewage to get the photos.
  • Call for emergency service right away rather than attempting to clear it yourself.

Why Rowhomes Are More Prone to This

A single-family home on its own lot typically has its own dedicated sewer lateral running straight to the municipal main. A lot of Chester's rowhomes, built close together during the early-1900s construction boom, were sometimes connected to shared or closely adjacent lateral lines, a construction pattern common in dense rowhome and twin-home development of that era across the Northeast. When a shared or nearby section fails, more than one household can be affected by a single problem, which is part of why sewer backups in Chester's older housing sometimes involve more than one property at once.

Beyond the shared-lateral pattern, the age of the pipe material itself matters just as much. Original clay or cast iron laterals from this construction era are prone to root intrusion at their joints and to cracking as soil shifts around them after more than a century underground, both real, checkable reasons this is a common call here.

How a Backup Actually Gets Diagnosed and Fixed

Camera Inspection

A sewer camera confirms exactly where a blockage, crack, or root intrusion sits before any repair work is scoped.

Clearing the Immediate Blockage

Cabling or hydro jetting clears the active backup first, restoring flow while the underlying cause is assessed.

Addressing the Root Cause

If the camera finds a structural problem rather than just buildup, that becomes a repair, spot repair, trenchless lining, or full replacement depending on severity.

Preventing the Next One

A backup that happened once is worth taking seriously even after it is cleared, since the underlying cause, whether root intrusion, a cracked joint, or an aging shared lateral, does not resolve itself. A camera inspection after a first backup gives you real information about whether this was a one-time event or an early sign of a developing problem that is worth addressing before it happens again, likely at a less convenient time.

If a Neighbor's Backup Might Be Connected to Yours

Because some of Chester's older rowhomes share or sit close to a common lateral section, a backup at a neighboring property is sometimes worth mentioning to your own plumber, even if your own drains seem fine at the moment. A camera inspection can trace the shared section and confirm whether the same underlying issue, a root mass at a particular joint, for instance, is likely to affect your property next, or whether the two homes' laterals are actually independent despite their proximity.

This kind of shared-infrastructure situation also affects who is responsible for a given repair. A blockage or crack within your own property's lateral section is generally your responsibility; one within the shared portion, or on the municipal side of the connection, may involve the city or a neighboring property owner. A camera inspection with clear footage is genuinely useful documentation for sorting that out, not just for planning the repair itself.

What Makes This Different From a Simple Drain Clog

A single slow or clogged fixture drain is a routine, inexpensive fix in almost every case. A true sewer backup, water and waste actually coming back up through a drain or toilet rather than just draining slowly, is a different category of problem entirely, both in terms of urgency and in terms of what is actually happening in the pipe. The distinguishing sign is usually which fixture backs up first: a backup at the lowest drain in the house, often a basement floor drain or a downstairs bathroom, is a classic indicator that the problem sits in the main line rather than in that one fixture's own branch line.

Treating a genuine backup with the same DIY approach as a simple clog, repeated plunging or an over-the-counter drain chemical, wastes time and can occasionally make the underlying problem worse, since neither addresses a blockage or structural issue in the main lateral itself, and repeated plunging on a true main-line backup can, in rare cases, push a hard blockage further into the line rather than clearing it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a sewer backup actually a health hazard, or just messy?

It is a genuine health hazard. Backed-up sewage carries bacteria and other pathogens, so any standing water from a backup should be treated as contaminated and avoided rather than cleaned up without proper precautions.

Why do multiple houses on my Chester block sometimes back up at the same time?

Some of Chester's older rowhomes were built with shared or closely adjacent sewer laterals, a common construction pattern in dense early-1900s rowhome development. A single failure in that shared section can affect more than one property at once.

Will a camera inspection tell me if the problem will happen again?

It gives real information about the pipe's actual condition, root intrusion, cracks, or offset joints, which is the best available basis for judging whether a backup was a one-time event or a sign of a developing structural issue.

Is sewer backup cleanup covered by homeowners insurance?

This varies significantly by policy; many standard policies exclude sewer backup unless a specific endorsement was added. Check your own policy directly, and keep documentation from the cleanup and repair for any claim.
