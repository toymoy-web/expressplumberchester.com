What Actually Counts as a Plumbing Emergency? A Chester Homeowner's Guide

Not every plumbing problem needs an immediate call, but some genuinely can't wait. Here's a practical way to tell the difference.

By the Chester Express Plumbing team · Serving Chester, PA

The Real Question: Is Water or Gas Actively Escaping?

The simplest practical test for whether something is a true plumbing emergency is whether water or gas is actively escaping right now, with no way to stop it yourself. A burst pipe, a water heater leaking heavily at the base, a toilet overflowing continuously, or any suspected gas smell all meet this bar and warrant an immediate call, day or night. A slow drip, a running toilet, or a clog in a single fixture, while worth addressing soon, generally does not.

This distinction matters because it changes what you should actually do in the moment. An active leak or gas smell calls for immediate action, shutting off the relevant valve if you can reach it safely, then calling right away. A non-urgent issue is better served by a scheduled appointment, which is often available same-day anyway without needing after-hours pricing.

True Emergencies vs. Things That Can Wait

True Emergency

Burst pipe, active major leak, sewage backup, no water at all, suspected gas leak, water heater leaking heavily.

Same-Day, Not Middle-of-the-Night

A single slow drain, a running toilet, low water pressure at one fixture, a minor drip under a sink.

Can Reasonably Be Scheduled

A fixture upgrade, a planned repipe, non-urgent water heater replacement, filtration or softener installation.

What to Do While You Decide

  • If water is actively flowing where it shouldn't, shut off the main water valve first, then assess.
  • If you smell gas, leave the house immediately and call from outside; do not investigate indoors.
  • For anything short of active water or gas escaping, take a few minutes to describe the issue clearly before calling, which helps a technician give you an accurate read on urgency.
  • When genuinely unsure, call anyway. A quick phone conversation can usually sort out urgency faster than trying to decide alone.

Why This Distinction Actually Saves You Money

Emergency response exists for genuine emergencies, and treating every plumbing issue as one, understandably, out of caution, can mean paying for after-hours urgency on a problem that would have been handled identically, at the identical price, during a scheduled same-day visit. On the flip side, treating an actual emergency as something that can wait risks meaningfully more water damage and a larger eventual repair. Learning the real difference, or simply calling and asking when unsure, is the most cost-effective approach either way.

A Few Scenarios Worth Walking Through

Consider a toilet that will not stop running: annoying and wasteful, but not actively causing damage, so a scheduled repair is entirely reasonable even if it means a day or two of a higher water bill in the meantime. Compare that to a toilet supply line that has disconnected and is spraying water into the bathroom: functionally similar fixture, completely different urgency, since active water damage is accumulating by the minute in the second scenario and not at all in the first.

Or consider a water heater that has simply stopped producing hot water versus one that is actively leaking a significant amount from its base. The first is an inconvenience worth scheduling promptly; the second is actively damaging your home's structure and belongings and warrants an immediate call regardless of the hour.

What Happens When You Call, Emergency or Not

Either way, the call itself looks similar: describe what you are seeing, including whether water or gas is actively escaping, how long it has been happening, and whether you have already shut off any relevant valve. According to standard emergency dispatch practice, that information lets whoever answers the phone give you an honest read on urgency and, for a genuine emergency, dispatch a technician immediately rather than scheduling for the next available slot.

There is no penalty for calling about something that turns out not to be urgent. A quick conversation costs you nothing and gets you a straight answer, which beats guessing wrong in either direction, either paying for unnecessary emergency service or leaving an actual emergency unaddressed overnight. When in doubt, treat the phone call itself as the low-cost first step, not the emergency dispatch decision, and let whoever answers help you sort out what actually happens next, whether that means a technician heading out immediately or a same-day appointment scheduled for later, either of which beats sitting with an unresolved question about your own safety or your home's condition, especially overnight when the temptation to just wait until morning is strongest and the risk of doing so is hardest to judge alone.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a running toilet a plumbing emergency?

Generally no. It wastes water and is worth fixing soon, but it is not actively causing damage the way a burst pipe or major leak would, so it can typically be scheduled rather than treated as an emergency call.

What should I do first if I'm not sure whether something is an emergency?

If water or gas is actively escaping with no way for you to stop it, treat it as an emergency and call right away. If you're genuinely unsure, calling and describing the situation is faster and more reliable than trying to decide alone.

Does calling for a non-emergency issue after hours cost extra?

Emergency and after-hours calls typically involve different pricing than a scheduled same-day appointment, so a non-urgent issue is usually more cost-effective handled during normal scheduling rather than treated as an emergency.

Is a slow leak under a sink urgent?

It's worth addressing promptly to avoid cabinet or flooring damage over time, but it is generally not an emergency requiring an immediate after-hours call unless it is actively worsening or causing visible damage right now.
