The Most Common Disposal Problems

A disposal that hums but doesn't spin is almost always jammed, not electrically dead, and often clears with a proper reset rather than a replacement. A disposal leaking from underneath usually means a worn seal, either at the sink flange or the discharge pipe connection, rather than a problem with the motor itself.

A disposal original to an older Chester kitchen renovation, sometimes 10 to 15 years old, is a realistic candidate for replacement rather than repair once the motor itself starts failing, since replacement parts for an older model are often harder to source than simply installing a current unit. Repair or Replace

Jammed Disposal

Cleared with an Allen wrench reset from underneath the unit; usually resolved on the spot without replacing anything.

Leaking Disposal

A worn seal at the sink flange or discharge connection is replaced; the disposal itself often doesn't need to come out.

Dead Motor

A disposal with a failed motor, especially an older unit, is typically replaced rather than repaired, since motor parts are rarely sold separately.

Signs Your Disposal Needs Attention

  • A humming sound with no spinning when you flip the switch.
  • Water pooling in the cabinet under the sink after running the disposal.
  • A burning smell, which can mean the motor is overheating or has failed.
  • Frequent jamming even with normal use and no unusual items going down the drain.
  • Unusual grinding noise or vibration compared to how it used to run.

Installing a New Disposal the Right Way

A new disposal needs to match the sink's drain flange size, the cabinet's available depth, and the kitchen's electrical setup, whether it's hardwired, plugged into an outlet, or run through a wall switch. Skipping that check is how a disposal that looks fine in the box ends up not fitting the space or drawing more power than the existing wiring is rated for.

For a dishwasher connected through the disposal, the knockout plug inside the disposal has to actually be removed before that connection is made; a disposal installed without that step done correctly is a common, avoidable cause of a dishwasher that won't drain properly afterward.

Horsepower matters more than most homeowners expect when replacing an older unit. A heavier-duty motor handles fibrous scraps and normal food waste with less jamming than a builder-grade unit from a decade or two ago, which is worth factoring into the replacement decision rather than defaulting to the cheapest available model.

A disposal installed under an older cast iron kitchen drain sometimes needs an adapter fitting to connect properly, since the outlet size and material differ from the PVC most modern disposals are designed to connect to directly. That gets confirmed before installation day, not discovered as a surprise once the old unit is already removed.

Air switch and wall switch setups both remain common in Chester kitchens depending on when the disposal was last replaced, and matching the new unit to whichever control style is already wired in avoids adding electrical work to what would otherwise be a straightforward swap. Installation follows manufacturer guidelines for mounting and electrical connection on every unit, gas or electric fittings aside, so warranty coverage on a new disposal stays valid.

A brief warranty check is worth doing before any repair too, since a disposal still under manufacturer coverage may be replaced at little or no cost through that warranty rather than paid for as an out-of-pocket repair.

Frequently Asked Questions

My disposal hums but won't spin, what does that mean?

A humming disposal is almost always jammed rather than electrically dead. It usually clears with a manual reset using the Allen wrench slot underneath the unit, without needing a replacement.

Why is my disposal leaking underneath the sink?

Most leaks come from a worn seal at the sink flange or the discharge pipe connection, not the motor housing itself. The seal can usually be replaced without removing the whole disposal.

How long should a garbage disposal last?

Most disposals last 8 to 12 years with normal use. A disposal older than that with a failing motor is usually more practical to replace than repair, since motor parts are rarely available separately.

Do you install customer-supplied disposals?

Yes, we can install a disposal you've already purchased or recommend one that fits your kitchen's plumbing and switch setup.
