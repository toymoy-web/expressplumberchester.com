Keeps Clogging No Matter How Often You Snake It?

Most people who end up needing hydro jetting don't search for it by name at first. What they know is that a cable clears the clog, and a few weeks later it's back, sometimes worse. That pattern almost always means the pipe has a buildup problem a cable can't fully solve: grease coating the pipe wall, root intrusion at a joint, or years of scale narrowing the usable diameter.

Hydro jetting uses high-pressure water, not a mechanical blade, to scour the entire inside surface of the pipe rather than just punching a hole through the middle of the blockage the way a cable does.

2

How Hydro Jetting Works

Camera Inspection First

A camera confirms the pipe's condition and material before jetting, since older or already-compromised pipe needs a lower pressure setting than sound PVC.

High-Pressure Water Jetting

A specialized nozzle sends water through the line at pressure calibrated to the pipe's material and condition, cutting through grease and root mass and flushing it out completely.

Follow-Up Camera Check

A second camera pass confirms the line is fully clear, not just passable, which is the real difference between jetting and a cable run.

When Hydro Jetting Is the Right Call

  • A clog that returns within a few weeks of being cabled, more than once.
  • Grease buildup in a kitchen line, especially in older cast iron drains common in Chester.
  • Root intrusion confirmed by a camera inspection in a sewer lateral.
  • Slow drainage across multiple fixtures rather than a single complete blockage.
  • A commercial kitchen line needing routine grease-trap maintenance.

Hydro Jetting vs. Standard Cabling

Cabling is the right first step for a single, isolated clog and costs less. Jetting is the right call once a line has shown it clogs repeatedly, because it addresses the buildup on the pipe wall itself rather than just clearing a path through the current blockage. Jetting is not the right tool for every pipe. Older, already-cracked, or severely deteriorated cast iron or clay pipe can be damaged by full-pressure jetting, which is why the camera inspection happens first and the pressure gets adjusted, or a different repair gets recommended instead, when the line's condition calls for it.

How Often a Line Needs Rejetting

A kitchen line with regular grease buildup, especially in an older Chester rowhome's original cast iron drain, may need jetting every one to two years to stay ahead of buildup rather than waiting for another full clog. A main sewer lateral with root intrusion typically needs rejetting on a longer cycle unless the underlying root source, usually a nearby tree, keeps regrowing into the same joint.

Jetting isn't a permanent fix for root intrusion on its own. It's a maintenance tool that buys time between clogs. If roots keep coming back at the same joint, that points toward an actual line repair rather than repeated jetting as the long-term answer.

Frequently Asked Questions

What's the difference between hydro jetting and snaking?

Snaking uses a rotating cable to punch through a blockage. Hydro jetting uses high-pressure water to scour the entire inside wall of the pipe, removing buildup that snaking leaves behind, which is why jetting handles a recurring clog that cabling alone can't fix.

Can hydro jetting damage old pipes?

It can, if the pipe is already cracked or severely deteriorated, which is why a camera inspection happens before jetting and the pressure is adjusted to the pipe's actual condition and material.

How do I know if I need jetting instead of another cabling?

If the same section clogs again within a few weeks of being cabled, especially more than once, that pattern points to buildup a cable can't fully clear, and jetting is usually the more permanent fix.

Do you offer emergency hydro jetting for a backed-up main line?

Yes, an active main line backup is treated as an emergency call on our 24/7 line.
