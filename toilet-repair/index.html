The Toilet Problems We Repair Most

Toilet calls in Chester fall into a handful of repeat categories: a toilet that runs continuously or refills on its own, a wax ring failure that lets water seep out at the base, a cracked tank or bowl, and a toilet that clogs repeatedly even after plunging. A toilet rocking slightly when you sit down is often an early sign of a failing wax ring, not just a loose bolt.

In Chester's older rowhomes, low-clearance bathrooms and tight supply-line routing mean a straightforward-looking toilet swap sometimes needs a slightly different fitting than a big-box store's standard kit assumes, which is worth confirming before a DIY attempt turns into a bigger repair.

Repair or Full Replacement

Running or Refilling Toilet

Almost always a fill valve or flapper issue, repaired in a single visit without replacing the toilet itself.

Leaking at the Base

Usually a failed wax ring; the toilet is pulled, the ring replaced, and the floor checked for water damage underneath before resetting it.

Cracked Tank or Bowl

A crack in porcelain cannot be safely repaired long-term; replacement is the honest recommendation once a crack is confirmed.

What to Do If Your Toilet Won't Stop Clogging

  • Avoid repeated flushing attempts, which can push a clog further in or cause an overflow.
  • Check whether other drains in the house are also slow, a sign the issue may be further down the line rather than the toilet itself.
  • Turn off the shutoff valve behind the toilet if the bowl is close to overflowing.
  • If a plunger doesn't clear it within a couple of tries, further attempts rarely help and a cable is usually needed.

When a Toilet Clog Is Really a Sewer Line Issue

A single toilet that clogs occasionally is normal wear. A toilet that clogs repeatedly, especially if it's the lowest fixture in the house, can point to a main line problem rather than the toilet itself; our sewer line repair team runs a camera inspection when that pattern shows up instead of replacing a toilet that isn't actually the cause.

Fitting an Older Bathroom Correctly

A lot of Chester's rowhome bathrooms were built around a toilet's rough-in measurement that isn't the modern 12-inch standard, which is why a toilet bought off the shelf without checking that measurement first sometimes doesn't sit flush against the wall or line up with the existing supply line. A technician checks this before ordering or installing a replacement, not after it's already in the bathroom.

Floor condition around the base matters too. If a wax ring has been leaking for a while before anyone notices, the subfloor underneath can soften, and that gets checked and addressed during the same repair rather than covered up by a new ring on a floor that needs attention first.

Supply line shutoff valves behind older toilets are worth checking during any repair visit as well, since a valve that's seized from years of not being turned can turn a five-minute part swap into a longer job if it fails the moment it's finally operated.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does my toilet keep running after I flush?

A continuously running toilet is almost always a worn fill valve or flapper that isn't sealing properly. It's a quick, inexpensive repair and also one of the more wasteful problems to leave alone since it runs water constantly.

Is it normal for a toilet to rock slightly?

No. A rocking toilet usually means the wax ring seal at the base has failed or the mounting bolts have loosened, both of which can let water leak onto the floor underneath if left alone.

Should I repair or replace an older toilet?

A running or leaking toilet with an intact tank and bowl is usually worth repairing. A cracked tank or bowl, or a toilet original to a home built decades ago, is often better replaced outright.

Do you offer 24/7 emergency toilet repair?

Yes, particularly for a toilet leaking at the base or overflowing, both of which are treated as emergency calls on our 24/7 line.
