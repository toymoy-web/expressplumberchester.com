What Drives This Call in Chester

Most tub and shower installation calls come from one of two situations: a cracked, chipped, or leaking original tub in an older Chester bathroom that's simply reached the end of its life, or a household wanting to convert a tub to a walk-in shower for easier access. Both involve the same core plumbing work, disconnecting and resetting supply and drain connections, even though the finish carpentry and tile work differ. Rowhomes along Lamokin Street and other blocks dating to Chester's early-1900s industrial boom are typical examples.

Older Chester bathrooms, often built to tighter dimensions than modern construction, sometimes need the rough-in plumbing adjusted slightly to fit a modern fixture's exact specifications, which is confirmed during an on-site assessment rather than assumed from measurements alone.

2

Installation Options

Standard Tub Replacement

A like-for-like tub swap using the existing drain and supply locations, the most straightforward and least expensive option.

Tub-to-Shower Conversion

Removing a tub for a walk-in shower involves relocating the drain to a shower base and adjusting supply lines for a shower valve and head.

Walk-In Tub Installation

For accessibility needs, a walk-in tub requires its own plumbing considerations, including a faster-draining setup given the door-seal design.

The Plumbing Side of the Project

Our role covers disconnecting the old fixture, adjusting or relocating drain and supply lines as needed, installing the new valve and fixture, and pressure-testing all connections before the space is closed up. Coordination with a tile installer or general contractor handling the surrounding finish work is a normal part of these projects rather than something the homeowner has to manage separately.

A Note on Waterproofing

Proper waterproofing behind the tub or shower surround is what actually prevents water damage over the fixture's lifetime, not just the fixture and plumbing connections themselves. A rough-in that isn't paired with correctly installed waterproof backing behind it can lead to hidden water damage inside the wall years down the line, which is why this gets confirmed as part of the project scope even though it technically falls on the finish-work side rather than the plumbing itself.

For a tub-to-shower conversion specifically, slope of the new shower base toward the drain is worth double-checking before tile goes down, since a base that doesn't drain correctly leads to standing water complaints that are far more expensive to fix after the tile is set than before.

What to Check Before You Commit to a Conversion

  • Confirm the existing drain location works for a shower base, or budget for relocation if it doesn't.
  • Check that your water heater and supply lines can support the flow rate of the new shower valve and head.
  • Decide on shower base material (fiberglass, acrylic, or tile) before plumbing rough-in, since drain placement can vary.
  • For a walk-in tub, confirm floor structure can support the added weight when filled with water.
  • Add grab bar or bench blocking to the wall framing at rough-in time even if not installed immediately, since it costs very little while the wall is already open and simplifies a future accessibility upgrade considerably compared to retrofitting solid backing into a finished wall later, an upgrade many homeowners end up wanting years after the original installation was completed and would rather not repeat.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you convert my bathtub to a walk-in shower?

Yes, this typically involves relocating the drain to the new shower base location and adjusting supply lines for the shower valve, which we handle as part of the plumbing scope of the project.

Do you do the tile and finish work too, or just the plumbing?

Our scope covers the plumbing: fixture removal, rough-in adjustments, valve and fixture installation, and pressure testing. We coordinate with a tile installer or general contractor for the surrounding finish work.

Will an older Chester bathroom's plumbing work with a modern shower fixture?

Usually with some adjustment. Older rough-in dimensions sometimes need minor changes to fit a modern fixture's exact specifications, which is confirmed during an on-site assessment.

How long does a tub-to-shower conversion take?

The plumbing portion is typically completed in a single day; the full project timeline depends on tile and finish work scheduled separately.
