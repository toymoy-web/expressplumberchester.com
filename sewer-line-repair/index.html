When a Clog Is Actually a Sewer Line Problem

Chester's sewer laterals are old enough, in a lot of cases original to homes built in the early 1900s, that root intrusion and settling are common rather than rare. A localized crack with root intrusion is one of the most frequent findings on our camera inspections here, and it is a repair, not something a cable or jetting pass will permanently fix.

Widener University's campus sits in the middle of this same older housing stock, built in the same early-1900s wave.

The clearest sign a clog is actually a line problem is repetition: if the same section keeps backing up within weeks of being cleared, or if our drain cleaning team's camera inspection finds a cracked joint, offset pipe, or heavy root intrusion, that moves the job from cleaning to repair.

Repair Options, From Least to Most Invasive

Spot Repair

A single cracked or root-damaged section is excavated and replaced at that specific point, leaving the rest of the line untouched.

Trenchless Pipe Lining

For a line with multiple weak points but a mostly intact structure, a liner is installed inside the existing pipe without full-length excavation.

Full Line Replacement

A lateral that has settled, collapsed, or failed across most of its length is replaced in full, usually the right call for the oldest original lines in the city.

Why Chester's Older Laterals Fail More Often

A large share of Chester's sewer laterals date to the same early-1900s construction boom as the housing above them, built from clay or unreinforced cast iron before modern PVC became standard. Both materials are more prone to root intrusion at their joints and to cracking as ground settles around them over a century of use, which is the direct reason sewer line repair shows up so often in our Chester call volume rather than being an upsell.

Signs You May Need Sewer Line Repair

  • Multiple drains back up at the same time, especially the lowest fixture in the house.
  • Sewage odor in the yard or near a cleanout, particularly after rain.
  • A soggy or unusually green patch of lawn above the suspected line path.
  • A clog that returns within weeks of being professionally cleared.
  • Gurgling from a toilet when a washing machine or dishwasher drains.

What to Expect on the Day of a Camera Inspection

A technician runs a sewer camera from the nearest cleanout or, if none exists, from a fixture drain, recording footage of the full line length that can be reviewed with you on site rather than described secondhand. The footage shows exactly where a crack, root mass, or offset joint sits and roughly how far it is from the house, which is what turns into the repair scope and price.

In homes without an existing cleanout, a common situation in Chester's older housing stock, installing one during the same visit makes future inspections and any repair work considerably easier, and it is worth asking about even if it is not the reason for today's call.

Emergency Steps During a Sewage Backup

  • Avoid direct contact with backed-up sewage. It carries bacteria and other pathogens, so treat any standing water from a backup as contaminated.
  • Do not run additional water down drains, showers, or toilets while a backup is active, since that only adds volume to a system that is already blocked.
  • Keep children and pets away from the affected area until it has been cleared and cleaned.
  • Document the backup with photos before cleanup for any insurance claim, but avoid wading through standing sewage to get them.

When a Sewer Problem Is an Emergency

Sewage coming up through a floor drain, tub, or toilet, several fixtures gurgling at once, or water showing up at basement drains after you run a washer all point to a blockage in the main line. These are emergency calls because sewage is a health hazard and damages finishes quickly. Stop using water in the house and call our 24/7 line.

The technician clears the blockage enough to stop the backup, then runs a camera to find out whether it was roots, grease, a collapsed section, or an old clay joint. You get the findings and an upfront price for a permanent fix before we start.

Main Line Cleaning and Camera Locating

Many sewer calls begin as a main line cleaning. Cabling clears the stoppage, and a camera inspection tells you whether it will come back. Where the line is cracked or offset, the camera also locates the break and its depth so the repair is scoped accurately.

If the line keeps clogging despite cabling, hydro jetting scours the pipe wall, and drain cleaning covers the lighter clogs that do not involve the main line.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I know if I need a sewer line repair instead of drain cleaning?

If a clog returns within weeks of being professionally cleared, or a camera inspection finds a cracked joint, offset section, or heavy root intrusion, that is a repair job rather than a cleaning.

Do you have to dig up my whole yard to repair a sewer line?

Not always. A localized crack can often be fixed with a spot repair at that one location, and trenchless lining can address multiple weak points without full-length excavation. A camera inspection determines which option fits.

Why do Chester homes have more sewer line problems than newer suburbs?

Many Chester sewer laterals date to the city's early-1900s construction boom and are made of clay or unreinforced cast iron, both more prone to root intrusion and cracking after a century of use than modern PVC lines.

Is a sewer backup an emergency?

Yes. An active backup into the house is treated as an emergency call on our 24/7 line, since it is a health and property risk, not something to schedule for later in the week.

Is a sewer backup an emergency?

Yes. Sewage in the house is a health hazard and spreads quickly. Stop running water, keep people and pets out of the affected area, and call (610) 672-7601 any hour.

Can you tell me where the break in my sewer line is?

Yes. A sewer camera shows the pipe condition and locates a break, root intrusion, or offset joint, so the repair can be limited to the failed section.
