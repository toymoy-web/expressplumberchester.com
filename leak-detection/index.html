Not Sure Where the Water Is Coming From?

Most leak detection calls don't start with visible dripping. A homeowner notices a higher water bill, a damp patch on a basement wall, or a musty smell near a bathroom, with nothing obviously wrong. That is exactly what leak detection is for: locating a hidden leak behind a wall, under a slab, or along a buried supply line without opening up the whole area to find it.

In Chester's older rowhomes and twins, these calls usually trace back to original galvanized supply lines or a soldered joint that finally failed after decades of use. If your water meter keeps moving with every fixture in the house turned off, that is the clearest sign of a hidden leak somewhere between the meter and your plumbing. How We Find a Hidden Leak

Chester Express Plumbing uses acoustic listening equipment and pressure testing to pinpoint a leak's location instead of guessing where to cut. This works on leaks under concrete slabs, inside finished walls, and along underground water service lines, the three most common hidden-leak locations in Chester's housing stock.

A technician isolates sections of the plumbing system and checks for pressure drop, narrowing the search to a specific wall section or a short run of pipe rather than an entire room. Most leaks are pinpointed in a single visit. If the source turns out to be the water heater itself rather than a supply line, our water heater repair team can usually handle it on the same trip.

What to Do If You Suspect a Hidden Leak

  • Your water bill has gone up noticeably with no change in household water use.
  • The water meter still moves when every faucet, toilet, and appliance in the house is off.
  • A warm spot on the floor over a slab foundation, not near a heating vent.
  • A musty smell or discolored patch on a wall or ceiling with no visible dripping.
  • Low water pressure at one fixture while pressure elsewhere in the house stays normal.

What Happens After the Leak Is Found

Spot Repair

Most pinpointed leaks get a targeted repair at the exact location, without cutting into unaffected wall or slab.

Section Replacement

A supply line with multiple failure points, especially original galvanized pipe, is often more cost-effective to replace in that section than to keep patching.

Whole-Home Repiping

Homes with galvanized pipe near the end of its service life sometimes make more sense to repipe fully rather than chase leaks one at a time.

Why a Small Leak Is Worth Finding Early

According to the EPA's WaterSense program, the average household leak wastes more than 9,300 gallons of water a year, and roughly one in ten homes has a leak large enough to waste 50 gallons or more per day. A hidden leak inside a wall or under a slab does the same quiet damage a running toilet does to a water bill, plus the added risk of mold growth and structural water damage the longer it goes undetected.

Chester's Older Housing Stock Drives This Call

A large share of Chester's rowhomes and twins date to the city's early-1900s industrial boom, with tighter basement and crawlspace access than newer construction. Original galvanized supply lines and cast iron drains from that era are past their expected service life in many of these homes, which is the practical reason leak detection is one of our most-called services here rather than a general claim.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does my water meter keep running when everything is turned off?

A moving meter with every fixture off is one of the strongest signs of a hidden leak somewhere between the meter and your home's plumbing. Chester Express Plumbing pinpoints the exact location with electronic leak detection instead of guessing at which wall or slab section to open.

Can you find a leak without cutting into my walls or floor?

Yes. Acoustic and pressure-based leak detection narrows the search to a specific section before any cutting happens, so only the actual leak location gets opened.
