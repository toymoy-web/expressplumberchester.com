A Borough Woven Into Chester's City Grid

Upland is a borough of about 3,068 people that borders the city of Chester on its northeast, east, and south sides, with Chester Township to the west and the boroughs of Brookhaven and Parkside to the north. Chester Creek marks part of the southern boundary. Upland's blocks sit close enough to Chester's own that residents are zoned to the Chester-Upland School District, and much of the housing here was built during the same early-1900s industrial boom that produced Chester's rowhomes and twins.

That shared construction era means the same plumbing issues show up here as in Chester proper: original galvanized supply lines and cast iron drains well past their expected service life, and basements with the kind of exposed, under-insulated pipe runs that fail hard during a cold snap.

If You Have a Burst Pipe or Active Leak

  • Shut off the main water valve if you can reach it safely.
  • Turn off electricity to any area with standing water before touching outlets or appliances there.
  • Take a few photos of the damage before cleanup starts, for a homeowners insurance claim.
  • Call our 24/7 emergency line. A technician will confirm arrival timing on the phone.

Winterizing Older Upland Homes

  • Insulate exposed pipes in basements and crawlspaces before the first hard freeze each year.
  • Know where your main shutoff valve is before you need it in an emergency.
  • Let faucets drip slightly during extreme cold to relieve pressure in exposed lines.
  • Disconnect and drain outdoor hoses and spigots before winter.

Home to Some of Delaware County's Oldest Sites

Upland was settled by Swedish colonists as early as 1643 and was incorporated as its own borough in 1869. The Caleb Pusey House, completed in 1696 and among the oldest buildings still standing in the United States, sits here, along with the former Crozer Theological Seminary campus where Martin Luther King Jr. earned his divinity degree in 1951. Several sites tied to this history are listed on the National Register of Historic Places.

That long settlement history means Upland's building stock spans a wide range of ages in a small footprint, from colonial-era structures near the historic district to the early-1900s rowhomes and twins that make up most of the borough today, which is why a technician familiar with both eras is useful here rather than one who has only worked newer construction.
